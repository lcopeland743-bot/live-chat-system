01 / What changed?

Separate the result from the surprise.

Start with two comparisons: how the business performed versus its earlier results, and how that performance compared with a sourced expectation.

Those comparisons may tell different stories. A price move alone cannot establish which changed—or reveal everything investors had already priced in.

01 / THE NEXT QUESTION

Follow the physical questions.

An AI narrative invites questions about the infrastructure it would require. What would need to be built? Who would supply it? What evidence would show that plans are becoming operations?

02 / THE NEXT QUESTION

Ask who eventually pays.

Separate enthusiasm for a technology from the question of a business model. Who is the customer, what problem is being solved, and what would make that customer keep paying?

03 / THE NEXT QUESTION

Look for the missing link.

A compelling narrative can leave a gap between investment and results. Identify that gap. Then ask which document, disclosure or business development would help you understand it.

03 / Expectations & evidence

Put the surprise in context.

Compare the reported result with the expectation that preceded it.

  1. Read the announcement.
  2. Examine the expectation.
  3. Question the time frame.

Keep the source, measure and time frame attached to each comparison.

01ExpectationsDid the business change—or did the expectations?A headline and the reaction to it are different things. Start by separating them.

Read what was actually announced before interpreting the response. Compare the announcement with the earlier expectation only if that expectation has an identifiable source. A price reaction alone does not establish what changed inside a business.

What evidence would change your reading of the story?

02Time horizonsA strong narrative. But over what time frame?A long-term idea and a near-term event can be discussed as though they are the same story.

Identify the time frame attached to each claim. Distinguish an aspiration from a scheduled milestone, and a scheduled milestone from a completed result. If the narrative does not specify a time frame, keep that uncertainty visible.

Which part is happening, and which part is still an expectation?

03Business fundamentalsGrowth gets the headline. What sits underneath?Look beyond the label and ask what is being measured.

When reading a growth claim, check whether it refers to customers, sales, earnings or another measure. Then examine the comparison period and the definitions used. Different measures answer different questions; one does not tell the whole story.

Which measure would help you understand the business more clearly?

04 / What would change the view?

Test the explanation you favor.

Keep this question

What would change your mind?

A changed business and changed expectations can coexist. Test both before settling on one explanation.

  1. A different business result.

    Which reported measure would challenge your reading of the announcement?

  2. A different starting expectation.

    Would a sourced earlier forecast change how surprising the result looks?

  3. A different time horizon.

    Does the interpretation hold if the expected milestone takes longer?
