Data centers draw electricity where they are built, not where the software story is told. The infrastructure question is physical before it is financial.

05 / CAPACITY COMES BEFORE CERTAINTY

THE PHYSICAL ECONOMY ANSWERS SLOWLY

Capital is committed before demand is certain.

Physical infrastructure requires property, equipment, financing, construction, depreciation, energy, and operating discipline before utilization is known.

Public filings show AI and cloud expansion intersecting with large technical-infrastructure commitments and costs. They show spending and exposure—not profitable demand or guaranteed beneficiaries.

06 / WHAT IF THE BOTTLENECK MOVES?

The physical story has failure modes.

Intelligence could become cheaper faster than usage grows. Infrastructure could be built ahead of profitable demand. Efficiency, architecture, substitution, utilization, interconnection, or policy could move the bottleneck somewhere else.

07 / WHAT SHOULD INVESTORS EXAMINE NEXT?

LEAVE THE PREDICTION BEHIND

Ask better questions of the chain.

The point is not to name a winner. It is to understand which assumptions are carrying the weight.

  1. CAPABILITY OR PROVIDER CLAIM?What changed in the system—and what is still only a claim?
  2. WORKLOAD OR EXPERIMENT?When does a demonstration become useful work that can repeat?
  3. WHICH CONSTRAINT APPEARS FIRST?Compute, memory, cooling, power, grid, or the time to connect them?
  4. CAPACITY OR OPTIMISM?Which spending becomes utilized capacity, and who carries idle-time risk?
  5. WHAT WOULD CHANGE YOUR VIEW?A thesis is useful only when it can be tested—and revised.
