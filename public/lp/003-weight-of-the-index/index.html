02 / BREADTH HAS A WEIGHT

Many holdings do not mean equal influence.

The S&P 500 is broad by company count, but it is float-adjusted market-cap weighted. Larger companies occupy larger shares by design. Count and weight answer different questions.

03 / THE LOAD-BEARING FEW

A DATED OBSERVATION

A few positions can carry a large part of the frame.

S&P Dow Jones Indices reported that the ten largest S&P 500 companies represented almost 40% of the index by mid-2025. Its index page later displayed a 37.8% top-ten weight as of August 31, 2026.

04 / DIFFERENT NAMES, SHARED DRIVERS

Different companies may share the same drivers.

Holdings may share sector, factor, earnings, platform, or macro exposures despite their different names.

Any comparison needs a defined measure, period, and level of exposure.

05 / WHY THE WEIGHT EXISTS

Size can have a rationale.

In a market-cap-weighted index, concentration can follow from the construction rule; it does not by itself show that the index is broken.

Larger weights may reflect genuine scale and profitability. Understanding that exposure still matters.

06 / TEST THE CONCERN

What if the largest companies deserve their weight?

Concentration may reflect real earnings power. Owning many companies can still reduce single-name exposure. Equal weight changes size, value, momentum, sector, and rebalance exposures; it is a different design, not a universal remedy.
